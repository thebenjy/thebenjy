Welcome back to the show. Today we're digging into AI agents and what actually
makes them useful in production rather than just impressive in a demo. My guest
has spent the last two years shipping agentic systems at scale.

The first thing we talked about is the gap between a demo and a deployment. In a
demo you control the inputs, you cherry-pick the happy path, and nobody notices
when the agent quietly fails. In production, the long tail of weird inputs is
where everything breaks. The guest's rule of thumb: if you can't observe what
the agent did step by step, you can't operate it.

We spent a good chunk of time on tool use. The big insight was that giving an
agent fewer, better-described tools beats giving it a huge toolbox. Every extra
tool is another way for the model to get confused. They described collapsing
fourteen tools down to five and seeing reliability jump.

On evaluation, the guest was adamant that you need a real eval set built from
production traffic, not synthetic prompts. They run a nightly eval that replays
yesterday's hardest cases and flags regressions before they reach users. The
quote that stuck with me: "Vibes are not a test suite."

Finally we discussed cost. The counterintuitive takeaway is that a smaller model
with good retrieval and tight tools often beats a frontier model used naively,
both on cost and latency. They mentioned the book "Thinking in Systems" as a
mental model for debugging agent loops, and recommended the Latent Space podcast
for keeping up. Great conversation. We'll be back next week.
