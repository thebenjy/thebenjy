Thanks for tuning in. This week's episode is all about longevity and the
practical, boring habits that actually move the needle, versus the expensive
biohacks that mostly don't.

My guest is a researcher who studies healthspan. She opened by making a
distinction between lifespan, how long you live, and healthspan, how long you
live well. The goal, she argued, should be compressing the period of decline at
the end of life, not just adding years.

The biggest lever, unsurprisingly, is exercise, but the nuance was interesting.
Zone 2 cardio builds the aerobic base, and a smaller amount of high-intensity
work raises your ceiling. But she was emphatic that strength training is the
most underrated intervention for people over forty, because muscle mass and grip
strength predict mortality better than almost anything else.

On nutrition she was refreshingly undogmatic. Protein adequacy matters more than
the specific diet tribe you join. Most people, especially older adults, eat far
too little protein. She suggested anchoring meals around a protein target and
letting the rest sort itself out.

Sleep got its own segment. The takeaway: consistency of sleep timing matters as
much as duration, and alcohol is the most common silent wrecker of sleep quality.
She referenced the book "Outlive" by Peter Attia as a good starting point, and
cautioned listeners to be skeptical of supplements with glossy marketing and thin
evidence. A grounded, useful conversation. See you next week.
